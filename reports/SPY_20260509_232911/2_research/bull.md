## 5. Bear Counterpoint: “RSI Is 75, So SPY Is Overbought”

This is the strongest bearish argument, and it deserves to be taken seriously.

RSI at **75.50** does show short-term overbought conditions. I am not dismissing that. Chasing aggressively after a strong move can produce poor short-term entry timing.

But here is where I disagree with the bear interpretation: **overbought is not automatically bearish in a strong uptrend.**

In powerful markets, RSI can stay elevated for longer than skeptics expect. The report itself notes that SPY is in a “band riding” phase near the Bollinger upper band. That often happens during strong upside momentum.

The right conclusion is not “sell everything.”  
The right conclusion is: **hold existing positions and buy pullbacks instead of panic-selling strength.**

If SPY pulls back toward **723–724**, near the 10-day EMA, that would be a normal digestion move — not a breakdown.

Even a deeper pullback toward the **683–687** area would put SPY near the 50-day SMA and lower Bollinger band zone, potentially creating a stronger medium-term buying opportunity.

So yes, the bear is right that the market is hot short term. But the bear is wrong if they interpret that as a reason to abandon the trend.

## 6. Bear Counterpoint: “Price Is Near the Bollinger Upper Band”

Again, true — but incomplete.

The Bollinger upper band is **738.70**, and SPY recently traded as high as **736.13**. That means SPY is close to short-term resistance around **735–740**.

But in strong trends, price can ride the upper band. The report explicitly acknowledges this.

A move above **740** with a firm close would be a bullish breakout confirmation. That would suggest buyers are willing to absorb supply at the current resistance zone and push the market into a new leg higher.

So the level to watch is simple:

- Below **735–740**: some consolidation would be normal.
- Above **740**: upside continuation becomes more likely.
- Pullback to **723–724**: attractive tactical support.
- Breakdown below **680–685**: then we reassess the medium-term trend.

Until those support levels break, the benefit of the doubt belongs to the bulls.

## 7. Bear Counterpoint: “The Risk/Reward Is Not Ideal for New Buyers”

I partially agree — but that is not a bearish thesis. That is an entry-timing argument.

There is a difference between saying:

> “Do not chase after a sharp move.”

and saying:

> “The market is no longer attractive.”

The first is reasonable. The second is not supported by the data.

For existing investors, the case is straightforward: **stay invested.** The trend remains intact.

For new investors, the bull strategy is not to blindly buy at any price. It is to use structure:

- Add near **723–724** if SPY pulls back and stabilizes.
- Add on a confirmed breakout above **740**.
- Add more aggressively if a deeper reset reaches **683–687** and holds.
- Treat the **670** area around the 200-day SMA as the major long-term trend line.

That is a disciplined bullish framework, not reckless optimism.
