## Bull Thesis: This Is an Earnings Re-Rating Story, Not Just a Momentum Trade

Samsung’s current move is not happening in a vacuum. The stock is responding to a major improvement in the underlying business.

Look at the fundamentals:

- 2025 Q4 revenue: **93.84 trillion won**, up about **23.8% YoY**
- 2025 Q4 operating profit: **20.07 trillion won**, up about **209% YoY**
- 2025 Q4 net income: **19.29 trillion won**, up about **154.6% YoY**
- TTM net income: **83.36 trillion won**
- ROE: **18.85%**
- ROA: **10.23%**
- Cash + short-term investments: **125.85 trillion won**
- Total debt: only about **25.24 trillion won**
- Forward P/E: **5.30x**
- PEG ratio: **0.15**

That is not a weak company being bid up on hype. That is a global semiconductor giant whose profitability is accelerating sharply, while the market is still valuing it cheaply relative to expected growth.

The bear can say, “The stock is near highs.” My response is: yes — because the earnings cycle has turned.

## Growth Potential: AI Memory Demand Is a Direct Tailwind

The strongest part of the bull case is that Samsung sits directly in the path of the AI infrastructure buildout.

AI servers need enormous amounts of high-performance memory: DRAM, HBM, NAND, SSDs, and high-capacity storage. The reports highlight that **DRAM and NAND supply are tightening**, and Bernstein expects **Q2 2026 contract prices to rise more than previously expected**.

That matters because Samsung’s semiconductor business has huge operating leverage. When memory prices rise, revenue improves — but operating profit often improves even faster because fixed costs are already embedded.

We are already seeing this in the numbers. Operating profit rose from:

- **4.68 trillion won in 2025 Q2**
- to **12.17 trillion won in 2025 Q3**
- to **20.07 trillion won in 2025 Q4**

That is a dramatic profit recovery. If DRAM/NAND pricing continues to strengthen in 2026, earnings estimates may still be too low.

And the demand signals are real. AMD’s data center revenue reportedly grew **57% YoY**, and global AI infrastructure spending remains strong. This is not only a Samsung-specific story — it is a broader AI capex cycle, and Samsung is one of the few companies with the scale to supply it.

## Competitive Advantages: Scale, Balance Sheet, Diversification

The bear may argue that Samsung faces competition from SK Hynix in HBM, TSMC in foundry, and Micron in memory. That is true, but it misses Samsung’s broader advantage: **Samsung does not need to win every category to benefit massively from this cycle.**

Samsung has several structural strengths:

### 1. Global memory scale

Samsung is one of the world’s most important memory suppliers. In a market where DRAM and NAND are tightening, scale becomes power. Customers need reliable volume, and Samsung can provide it.

### 2. Massive financial flexibility

With **125.85 trillion won** in cash and short-term investments against only **25.24 trillion won** in total borrowings, Samsung has one of the strongest balance sheets in global technology.

That gives it the ability to:

- invest aggressively in HBM and advanced memory,
- sustain capex through cycles,
- maintain R&D intensity,
- buy back shares,
- pay dividends,
- and absorb macro shocks better than weaker competitors.

### 3. Diversified business model

Samsung is not just memory. It also has smartphones, displays, consumer electronics, wearables, and ecosystem products. The Withings health-data partnership may not move earnings immediately, but it shows Samsung continues to strengthen the Galaxy ecosystem against Apple, Google, Fitbit, and others.

That diversification gives Samsung more resilience than a pure-play memory company.

## Bear Counterpoint 1: “The Stock Is Overbought”

Fair. It is overbought short term.

But overbought is not the same as overvalued.

The stock is near its 52-week high of **277,000 won**, but the forward P/E is still only **5.30x**, and the PEG ratio is **0.15**. That is extremely inexpensive for a company with accelerating profits, AI exposure, strong cash flow, and a fortress balance sheet.

So the better strategy is not to avoid the stock completely. It is to accumulate intelligently:

- Buy pullbacks toward **260,000 won**
- Add more if it stabilizes around **251,000 won**
- Treat **243,000 won**, near the 10-day EMA, as a key trend support area
- If it breaks above **277,000 won** with volume above 40 million shares, that could confirm another upside leg toward **280,000–300,000 won**

The bear is right on timing caution. But that supports a staggered buying plan, not a bearish thesis.

---

## Bear Counterpoint 2: “Labor Disputes Could Hurt Production”

The union issue is a real short-term risk. Mediation on May 11–12 could create volatility.

But we should keep perspective. The report notes that despite the labor headlines, Samsung’s stock reportedly rose **2.6% in after-hours trading**. That tells us the market is currently prioritizing memory pricing and AI demand over labor risk.

Also, this is an event risk, not a structural impairment. If mediation progresses or a settlement is reached, the uncertainty could actually clear and become a positive catalyst.

The bear case would be stronger if labor disruption were already causing prolonged production damage. Based on the provided data, that is not the case yet.

---

## Bear Counterpoint 3: “Samsung Is Behind SK Hynix in HBM”

This is probably the bear’s best strategic argument — but it is still not enough to override the bull case.

Yes, SK Hynix has been viewed more favorably in HBM. But AI memory demand is not limited to one narrow product. The current reports emphasize broad **DRAM and NAND supply tightness**, not just HBM.

Samsung benefits from:

- server DRAM demand,
- high-capacity NAND demand,
- AI server storage demand,
- general memory pricing recovery,
- and potential HBM catch-up.

Also, Samsung’s balance sheet gives it enormous capacity to invest and close technology gaps. This is not a small player trying to survive. It is a global memory leader with the capital to compete.

The HBM concern may limit near-term valuation premium versus SK Hynix, but it does not destroy Samsung’s earnings recovery thesis.

---

## Bear Counterpoint 4: “Memory Price Increases Could Hurt Customers and Destroy Demand”

This is worth monitoring, especially with Sony and Nintendo citing memory cost pressure.

But for Samsung shareholders, the immediate takeaway is bullish: customers are confirming that memory prices are rising enough to affect their cost structures. That means suppliers have regained pricing power.

Could higher prices eventually slow demand? Yes. But we are not there yet. The current environment is characterized by:

- AI server demand expansion,
- tight supply,
- rising contract prices,
- improving semiconductor margins,
- and positive investor sentiment toward Asian chip leaders.

In other words, we are still in the phase where pricing power benefits Samsung more than it hurts end demand.

---

## Bear Counterpoint 5: “The Market Has Already Priced It In”

If Samsung were trading at 25x forward earnings, I would agree. But it is not.

At **5.30x forward earnings**, the market is not pricing Samsung like a fully valued AI winner. It is still applying a cyclical discount.

That creates an attractive setup: if investors increasingly view Samsung as a core AI infrastructure supplier rather than merely a cyclical memory stock, the valuation multiple can expand while earnings also rise.

That is the classic double benefit:

1. **Earnings upgrades**
2. **Multiple re-rating**

That is why the upside case remains compelling.

For long-term investors, the risk is not owning Samsung after a run. The bigger risk is underestimating how powerful this AI-driven memory cycle can become.
